Thread insertion robot

A simulated robot that picks up a 40 µm thread, drives it into a tissue phantom through a puncture, lets go at depth and moves on to the next site. Built in MuJoCo, with disturbances from the workcell, the sensors and the tissue, and prepared for learning with PufferLib.

Three sites under nominal disturbances, replayed from recorded MuJoCo states. The needle's work plays at 0.02× real time and the moves between sites at 0.2×; the thread is drawn in lime.

The setup

  • Gantry. X, Y and Z stages position the tool; a fast insertion carriage drives the needle.
  • Needle. A conical point, a Ø 50 µm tip section and a Ø 100 µm shaft.
  • Thread tube. A Ø 200 µm bore beside the needle, 15° from vertical, on an arm that moves with the tool.
  • Thread. 40 µm across and 8.25 mm long, waiting in the tube with its end on the needle's path.
  • Tissue phantom. A domed block with target rings and vessel markings, and a needle-tissue force model.

One insertion

  1. Stick. The needle comes down; when its point reaches the thread's end, the two stick.
  2. Insert. The needle carries the thread out of the tube and through a 2 mN puncture, at up to about 200 mm/s.
  3. Release. At 2 mm depth the thread lets go of the needle.
  4. Snap back. The needle withdraws; the tissue holds the thread.
  5. Lift and reload. The tool lifts 8 mm, clear of the thread left standing, and a new thread appears in the tube.

The same three sites without disturbances.

What is simulated, and what stands in

PartHow it is modelled
RobotMuJoCo rigid bodies with force-limited servo drives, stepped at 50 µs (RK4).
ThreadA discrete elastic rod (a published model for MuJoCo, arXiv 2310.00911, ported with a corrected force projection): 18 segments with bending and twisting stiffness. To run at 50 µs rather than 5 µs, its fastest wiggles are slowed with added joint inertia, and it has internal damping; its shape and sag are unchanged.
TissueA puncture model: dimpling to puncture at 2 mN, then cutting and shaft friction. Each thread segment that enters is gripped in place and slips above a limit. The parameters are illustrative until measured.
HandlingStand-ins: MuJoCo constraints make the needle stick to the thread's end, let go at depth, and hold the waiting thread in the tube. New threads are placed into the tube between sites. These replace a real cartridge and adhesion, which this project does not model.
SpeedOne three-site run, about 1.9 s of simulated time, takes about 41 s on one CPU core.

The thread is a 40 µm round rod; real threads are thin polymer ribbons. Cameras in these views are for observers only; the controller does not use pixels.

Disturbances

Every run can include a shared disturbance layer, the same one the alignment experiments used. Level 1 is a quality workcell: a precision stage on an isolation table. Level 2 doubles every magnitude.

GroupLevel 1 (nominal)
RobotSlowly varying force noise (up to 10 mN std), extra friction (up to 20 mN) and table vibration (1 mm/s², 5–60 Hz) on every axis.
SensingNeedle-tip latency up to 10 ms and 0.5 µm noise; target estimate with 3 µm noise, a 1 µm bias and slow drift.
TissueBreathing (0.2–0.4 Hz, up to 30 µm vertical) and pulse (1–2 Hz, up to 10 µm). The phantom moves, and the targets, the puncture model and the grip on placed threads move with it.

Three sites at level 2 (stress).

Baseline: the scripted yardstick

The yardstick measures the target, makes one correction from the measured needle tip, and inserts. It ran on evaluation seeds 1001–1010 at each level, fixed before any training.

Placement is the thread end's distance from the target ring's centre. Site 0 is on top of the dome; sites 5 and 1 are on its slope, where the yardstick is off by about 30 µm even without disturbances. That gap is the first thing to improve.

Toward training

  • What learns. The approach, the final correction and the insertion stroke, from measured needle tip and target, under the disturbance layer. The stand-in handling stays as it is.
  • Judged by. Every thread placed, at 2 mm depth, closer to its target than the yardstick, with no robot contact with the tissue, on evaluation seeds 1001–1010 at levels 1 and 2.
  • Speed comes first. At about 20 times slower than real time on one core, the full cycle is too slow to train on directly. The next step is a fast training environment, either a C core with the thread as a load on the needle or MuJoCo Warp on the RTX 4090, checked against this simulation before it is trusted.

How this was built

A harness, a brief, and two coding agents working in a loop

Two things made this possible: a harness and a brief. The harness was in place before this project began. It is a repository set up for simulation and learning work: MuJoCo for physics, PufferLib 5.0 for reinforcement learning, a locked Python environment, Git LFS for assets, a commit-based workflow between a laptop and a remote RTX 4090 workstation, earlier MuJoCo projects to build on, and written engineering rules for how simulations are modelled, tested, recorded and documented here. The brief states what to build and the gates the work must meet.

Everything specific to this robot was written by two coding agents working together inside that harness from the brief, one running GPT-6 Astra and one running Claude Opus 5.5. The project's first commit was on October 5.

  1. Brief and gateswhat to build, how it is judged
  2. Planthe next measurable step
  3. Buildcode, models, configuration
  4. Runsimulation or training
  5. Measureagainst the gates, on held-out seeds
  6. Recordresults and failures, committed
  7. Change one thingthen run again

We call this improvement loop AutoResearch. Getting this design to work took a series of recorded failures. A thread built off-centre in its tube was missed by the needle. Long rigid thread segments jammed at the tube's mouth. A 45° tube made the thread wrap the needle at insertion speed. A tissue spring on light segments diverged, and an undamped thread whipped after release. A person sets the brief, reviews every attempt on video and corrects course. In this design they rejected a pincher with no visible mounting, chose the thread tube from photos of a real insertion robot, removed the thread's loop, proposed shorter thread segments, and asked for a video of every attempt.

"Self-improving" means the work improves, not the agents. The code, the physics and the process get better through measured iterations, and every mistake stays on the record.
